// straight answers

The questions everyone asks first

We're a small team. Is this only for big companies?

It's the opposite. A large company can absorb repetitive admin by hiring; a lean team can't. The smaller the team, the more of its week is spent on work that doesn't need a person, which is exactly the work we take off it.

Do we have to replace the tools we already use?

No. We build into your current stack: your CRM, your spreadsheets, your inbox, your project tool. A migration is a cost with no upside for you, and it's the fastest way to make a project fail. If something genuinely can't be automated where it is, we'll tell you before you spend anything.

What happens to our data?

It stays in your systems, under your accounts. We work with the minimum access needed to build and run each automation, and that access is yours to revoke at any point. We'll write down exactly what each workflow reads and writes before it goes live.

What if the AI gets something wrong?

Assume it will, occasionally, so we design for it. Anything customer-facing or financial goes to a person for approval before it lands. Everything else logs what it did, so a mistake is visible and reversible instead of silent. And any workflow can be switched off without breaking the ones around it.

Will this replace people on our team?

Most of our clients aren't trying to cut headcount. They're trying to stop paying skilled people to retype things. The usual outcome is that the same team handles more without the week disappearing into admin. If you are looking to reduce headcount, that's a decision for you to make; it isn't the case we make for the work.

How is it priced, and how soon does something work?

We scope and price each build up front against the hours it's replacing, so you can see what it's worth before you commit. No open-ended retainers to find out. The mapping step comes first and is deliberately small, because neither of us can price the work honestly until we've seen where your time actually goes.
